1. Executive summary

What does Tanfeeth provide?

Tanfeeth is an internal platform that helps a government entity manage competitions, contracts and projects in one clear path, with precise permissions and a complete record of every action.

It integrates with Etimad and does not replace it. Etimad handles tendering, receiving offers and award, while Tanfeeth manages the entity's internal work, then follows the contract and project after signature.

Key value

  • Clear governance: an order or contract changes state only through an approved, authorized action.
  • Full follow-up: the platform shows project status, delays, letters, payments and contractor obligations.
  • Audit trail: the system records who performed an action, when, and the state before and after it.
  • AI assistant: the system suggests the right action and explains why, and the final decision stays with the responsible user.
Core principle: the AI does not automatically approve any financial, contractual or regulatory decision.

2. Product scope & boundaries

The relationship between Tanfeeth and Etimad

Etimad platform
  • Publishing the competition and receiving offers.
  • Award procedures.
  • May be the signing surface and source of contract data.
Tanfeeth platform
  • Order & kraasa preparation, internal approvals and committees.
  • Internal offer analysis and reflecting the signed contract.
  • After signature: project, contractor, letters, payments, amendments, acceptance.
The gap Tanfeeth fills: everything that comes after award and signature — which Etimad does not cover.

In scope

  • Managing orders, competitions, contracts and projects.
  • Approval workflow, assignment, signing and official correspondence.
  • Contractor portal, progress tracking and contract-action management.
  • Reports, dashboards, audit log and required integrations.

Out of current scope

  • Replacing Etimad or duplicating its official functions.
  • Executing AI decisions without human approval.
  • Blockchain, voice assistant and advanced capabilities before the core is complete.

6. End-to-end scenario

Delay, warning, withdrawal of works, and the completion contract

1
The project dashboard shows the contractor falling behind schedule.
2
The AI suggests issuing a warning and explains the reason.
3
Human decision: the supervising department reviews the suggestion and approves, edits or rejects it.
4
The system generates the warning letter, obtains a reference number, then sends it.
5
The contractor replies inside the platform within the set window.
6
Check: the department reviews the reply — if unresolved, a final warning is issued.
7
If the breach continues, withdrawal of works is approved per permissions.
8
The system creates a completion contract linked to the original and loads the remaining works into it.
9
All steps and decisions are recorded in the audit log.
Core acceptance criterion: this scenario runs end-to-end on a test contract, with permissions, correspondence, audit and completion-contract linking.

13. Cybersecurity & protection

Compliance with the Essential Cybersecurity Controls (ECC-2:2024) of the National Cybersecurity Authority

Tanfeeth complies with the Essential Cybersecurity Controls (ECC-2:2024): four main domains, 28 subdomains and 108 controls. Responsibility splits into controls the platform implements, organizational controls owned by the entity (the cybersecurity function), and shared controls with the cloud hosting provider.

Shared responsibility principle: Tanfeeth implements the technical controls in the platform and enables the entity to demonstrate compliance, while governance, policies, awareness and audit remain the entity's responsibility, and data-center security is the responsibility of the approved in-Kingdom hosting provider.

3 — Cybersecurity ResilienceBackup, recovery, continuity plan and RTO/RPO objectives.Platform + Hosting
4 — Third-Party & Cloud ComputingSupplier assessment, in-Kingdom data residency and the shared-responsibility model with the cloud provider.Platform + Provider
1 · Cybersecurity GovernancePolicies · Roles · Risk · Audit · HR · Awareness

4 · Third-Party & Cloud ComputingSupplier assessment · Data residency · Shared responsibility

What the platform builds (technical controls)

  • Data encryption at rest/in transit and key management; multi-factor authentication (MFA); precise permissions, least privilege, segregation of duties and privileged-access management.
  • Tamper-evident audit log and continuous monitoring via SIEM; tenant isolation and in-Kingdom data residency.
  • Web application firewall (WAF), HTTPS and multi-tier architecture; a secure development lifecycle (secure coding, vulnerability scanning, penetration testing).
  • Backup, recovery and continuity plan; cybersecurity incident management, response and reporting to the authority.
  • Email protection (SPF/DKIM/DMARC), network protection (IPS and DDoS protection) and vulnerability/patch management.

Entity responsibility (organizational controls)

  • Cybersecurity strategy and policies, risk management, awareness and training.
  • Cybersecurity in human resources, periodic audit and ECC self-assessment, and physical security.

15. Scenarios in detail

Covering every main and exceptional path

1 · Order creation & approval BR-01
Requesting dept creates the order → Head approves → Finance confirms the budget and applies document split above the threshold → Higher management approves. Alternatives: return-with-comment or reject-with-reason.
2 · Commencement — supply vs maintenance BR-02
Supply: commencement request → confirm → commencement letter → sign → reference number. Maintenance: adds a site-handover minute, the site committee and a cover letter.
3 · Default & withdrawal of works Critical
Delay detected → AI suggestion → human approval → warning letter (with reference) → contractor reply → final warning → withdrawal → completion contract linked to the original. (Detail in section 6.)
4 · Contract variation
Change request (value/duration/terms) → review & approval → bid examination if needed → signing → variation letter with reference → BOQ update.
5 · Extension, suspension & resumption
Request → approval → letter (extension/suspension/resumption) with reference → update project duration and status, with guarantee-validity check.
6 · Payments & dues
Payment due against the BOQ and budget availability → approval → dues letter → record the financial effect.
7 · Closeout & acceptance
Preliminary acceptance (committee + approval) → maintenance period → final acceptance → release of final guarantee → contract closeout.
8 · Contractor evaluation
Requesting dept evaluates → Head reviews → Higher management approves → the supplier reliability index is fed.
9 · Contract signing — two scenarios
A: signed in Etimad, then imported and reflected. B: signed outside Etimad by creating contract data from approved drafts with manager confirmation.
10 · Contractor onboarding & invitation
After signature: single-use invitation → accept & register → bind to commercial registration and contract → Nafath & GOSI verification → access limited to their contracts only.
Full coverage: these scenarios cover the main path, execution and exceptional paths, all as gated and audited transitions.

16. FAQ

Short answers to the most frequent questions

What is the difference between Tanfeeth and Etimad?
Etimad handles tendering, receiving offers and award. Tanfeeth manages the entity's internal work and owns the post-signature world: contractor, payments, warnings, amendments and acceptance.
Does Tanfeeth replace Etimad?
No; it complements and integrates with it, and does not duplicate its official functions.
Where is the data hosted?
Inside Saudi Arabia, with encryption and tenant isolation, in compliance with the National Cybersecurity Authority and the Communications Authority.
How is cybersecurity ensured?
By complying with the Essential Cybersecurity Controls (ECC) across their four domains, under a shared responsibility between the platform, the entity and the hosting provider (section 13).
Does the AI make automatic decisions?
No; any financial, contractual or regulatory decision requires explicit human review and approval (the human-in-the-loop principle); the AI only suggests and explains.
How does the contractor access the platform?
After signature, the contracts staff send a single-use invitation; the contractor registers and the account is bound to the commercial registration and contract, with identity verified via Nafath.
What happens when a contractor defaults?
The system detects the delay and suggests a warning, then a final warning, then withdrawal of works if it continues, and creates a completion contract linked to the original carrying the remaining scope.
How are permissions managed?
Role-based access control (RBAC); the contract manager holds permanent default access to the department's orders, and assignment transfers the action permission while the manager retains access.
Does it integrate with national platforms?
Yes: Etimad, Nafath, the Ministry of Finance and GOSI, plus e-signature and notification channels.
How are official letters issued?
From dynamic templates with merged contract data; no external letter is sent before obtaining a reference number and date.
What is the difference between a "project" and a "contract"?
The project is the work to be delivered; the contract is the signed instrument that governs it; project execution begins after the contract is signed.
How long does the first phase take?
The reference schedule is 24 weeks; the operational core (MVP) completes around week 16, and the schedule is re-baselined after the kickoff workshop.
Does the system support Arabic and English?
Yes, with full support for Arabic (RTL) and English, the Hijri and Gregorian calendars, and the Saudi Riyal.
Are all actions recorded?
Yes, in a tamper-evident audit log that keeps the actor, time and state before and after each action.
What is needed before starting?
Settling the decisions required (section 12) and holding a kickoff workshop to approve the scope, approval chains, integration readiness and the team.
